## The outcome

The owner stops wondering how the rollout is going and reads it. Four lines, the same every month: who in the role can open the workflow; how many of the month's cases went through it, week by week; how the reviewer's sample of its drafts sorted out, with the reasons grouped; and where the one number the workflow exists to move stands against the month before it started.

Then a short paragraph reads the lines side by side and says what they point at together. Wide access with thin use is a question about how the team was brought on. Steady use with drafts that keep being rewritten is a question about the workflow itself. Good use, good drafts and a business number that has not moved is a question about whether this was the right work to hand over. That is how a whole team comes onto a workflow, and how you 10X the ROI of your existing AI one workflow at a time.

The sheet measures the team's work as a whole. Nobody is counted, ranked or named, and nobody is singled out as a low user. A change in the business number is reported beside its baseline, never explained as caused by the workflow, and a single month is called one reading, never a trend. The owner reads the four lines and decides.

## Who starts the work, and when

The workflow's owner: the person in the team who keeps the workflow current, such as a credit manager or an intake lead. They prepare the first sheet at the end of the first full month after rollout, then every month, on the same inputs counted the same way. They bring it to the team's regular meeting.

## What they supply

- **The access confirmation from IT:** a short table by role saying how many people are in the role, how many can open the workflow, and whether it can read the records it needs. It is a list of access, not a record of anyone's use.
- **The team's work log for the month,** exported from the system the work is done in: one row per case handled, with its completion date, a yes or no for whether the workflow prepared it, and a note. The default column headers are listed in the workflow template; when an export's headers differ, the sheet stops and asks which column is which.
- **The reviewer's sample record:** each sampled draft marked sent as prepared or lightly edited, substantially rewritten, or stopped, with a one-line reason.
- **The one business number** the workflow exists to move, for the month and for the baseline month before it started, counted the same way.
- **The notes from the team's regular meeting** on what the workflow missed and when the team chose not to use it.
- **What else changed that month:** an absence, a new rate or price, a changed form, a seasonal rush.

If anyone supplies a usage record by person, or a log with a column naming who handled each case, the sheet stops and asks for team-level counts instead.

## What comes out

One sheet, in a fixed order:

1. **Installed: availability.** How many in each role can open the workflow, whether its records are confirmed, and the date of the team's first real case.
2. **Used: repeat use.** Cases handled, runs, and the share prepared with the workflow, for the month and for each week, with the direction from the first week to the last and the team's own notes on the cases done without it.
3. **Useful: quality.** The reviewer's sample sorted three ways, with the reasons for rewritten and stopped drafts grouped in the reviewer's words.
4. **Useful: business effect.** The month against the baseline, the change in points and the target, with the plain statement that one month is a reading and the sheet does not show what caused the change.
5. **Read the lines together:** a short paragraph using only the facts supplied, ending with what the lines point at: the rollout, the workflow, the choice of work, or nothing.
6. **What else changed,** with the line each change touches.
7. **Questions for the owner,** at most six, and a blank line for the owner's decisions.

Every count on the sheet can be recounted from the work log and the sample record.

## What has to change for your company

Choose the one business number, write down how it is counted, and count the baseline month before the workflow starts. Decide how a case is marked as prepared with the workflow in the system the work is done in, and write your column names down once so every month reads the same. Name who reviews the sample and how many drafts they take each month. Set your own reading lines if the defaults do not fit: 70% of cases in the last week, a fall of more than 10 points, 80% of drafts sent as prepared or lightly edited, and a move of 5 points on the business number. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from files and notes you supply; it needs no connection to any system.
