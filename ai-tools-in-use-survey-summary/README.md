## The outcome

The person writing the company's first AI plan starts from what is already happening, not from a guess. One summary shows, team by team, which assistants and AI features people use and how often, what they use them for in their own words, and which of those tools the company pays for, which people pay for themselves and which they use free. The seats the company buys sit beside the number of answers that say they use them.

The pieces of work people name most often become a ready list of candidates for the [opportunity scorecard](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/ai-opportunity-scorecard/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=workflow-collections&utm_content=operations-ai-tools-in-use-survey-summary&from=gh-ai-tools-in-use-survey-summary), so the plan begins where people have already found value. Answers that mention customer, personal or confidential information are set apart for the owner, by response id, with the company's own rule quoted above them. That section lists answers from teams too small to report one by one, because it is read only by the owner and by the person who runs the work and hands it over.

Nobody is named. A team with too few answers is counted only in the company total. The summary judges no one's use and decides nothing: the owner reads it and chooses what happens next.

## Who starts the work, and when

The owner, the operations lead or the office manager runs it once before the first AI plan is written, as soon as the survey closes, and again every six months with a fresh survey. The person writing the plan reads the summary, and only the owner, and the person who runs the work and hands it over, read the section of set-apart answers.

## What they supply

- **The response export from your survey tool**, one row per answer, with these columns: response_id, team, role, tools_used, how_often, what_for, paid_by (company, self, free), data_types_entered and what_would_help. The survey is answered without names. If a column header does not match, the workflow template stops and asks which column is which.
- **The list of AI subscriptions the company pays for,** from finance: each tool, its plan, the number of seats and the team they belong to.
- **The company's rule on what information may go into an assistant**, if it has one.
- **The team list,** with the number of people in each team.
- **The smallest group to report,** which is five answers unless the company sets a larger number.

It works from the files a person supplies. It needs no connection to the survey tool, and it never asks for a password, a login code or a key.

## What comes out

One summary in a fixed order: each team's tools and how often they are used, the company total, tools paid for by the company against tools people pay for themselves or use free, seats against answers (counts only), the pieces of work in people's own words, the answers set apart for the owner, the candidates for the opportunity scorecard, what people said would help, and the teams with too few answers to report. The counts are checked against each other so a missing row shows before anyone relies on the page.

Names that appear in people's own words are removed, and the role column is never printed. Every section is printed even when it is empty.

## What has to change for your company

Write the survey once, with the columns above, and keep it anonymous: no name or email question. Decide the smallest group you will report; five is the default, and a larger number protects people in small teams further. Bring the team list up to date on the day the survey closes. Write your rule on what may go into an assistant in a sentence or two, if you have one. Decide who reads the summary and who reads the set-apart answers; remove that section before the summary goes any further. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the exports you give it.

One risk to weigh before you share the summary beyond the owner: in the section of pieces of work, a piece named only by a team too small to report, such as a task only the finance team does, can point to that team even though the team is not named. Remove or generalize those quoted words before the summary goes further, or share only the counts. The summary gives no legal or security opinion and recommends no product. Whether a use fits your rule, and what follows, is for the owner and the person who answers for the company's obligations.
