## The outcome

The end of a pilot turns into a decision the leader can make on the facts. The leader who decides reads, on two pages, exactly what the pilot showed: how many cases were tested, which passed, which failed and for what reason, and how the workflow did on real work in the pilot weeks. Every count is shown as a number of cases, so a strong result on a handful of runs reads as a handful of runs.

Failures are grouped by the causes your own people wrote down when they judged them, with the cases behind each group, and a run someone had to correct is shown beside them rather than hidden among the passes. What nobody tested sits in its own section, so a gap is never mistaken for a pass. The bar the owner wrote before the pilot is set beside the results line by line, with the working for each.

Then the page lays out what the whole team would need: the access still missing, the instructions to fix, the records to tidy and who owns the workflow from here. The decision stays open as three options, roll out, change and re-test, or stop, each with what it would take. The write-up recommends none of them, claims no saving and no business effect, and names roles, never people.

## Who starts the work, and when

The pilot owner starts it at the end of the pilot period, once the last test run has been judged and the run log is closed: usually whoever leads the team that ran it, such as an operations lead, department head or team lead. The leader who decides whether the workflow goes to the whole team, such as the head of operations, reads it before the decision meeting. The expert who judged the test runs answers any questions it raises.

## What they supply

- **The acceptance test results:** each test case and run, what was expected, what came out, pass or fail, and the role that judged it, with the cause of each failure in the judge's words.
- **The run log from the pilot weeks:** each piece of real work the workflow prepared, its date and version, and whether it was used as prepared, edited or set aside, with the reason.
- **The bar the owner wrote before the pilot:** each line of what has to be true for the workflow to go to the whole team.
- **The list of what was deliberately not tested,** with why.
- **The people who took part, by role,** and what each role did.
- **Open items noted during the pilot,** on access, instructions and records, and **who owns the workflow afterwards:** business ownership, technical maintenance, user support and change approval.
- **The settings:** the final version, the pilot dates, the pilot owner, the leader who decides and the team it would go to.

## What comes out

One draft write-up in seven sections: what was tested and on how many cases; what passed; what failed, grouped by cause with the cases behind each; what was not tested and so is not known; the bar set beside the results, each line met, not met, not shown or left for the leader to judge; what extending the workflow to the whole team would take; and the decision as three options with what each would need. Numbered questions for the pilot owner follow.

When a column does not match, a result is recorded in words outside the agreed list, or the pilot has not ended, the work stops and asks. It never counts a failed or unjudged run as a pass, never writes a bar after the fact, and sends nothing.

## What has to change for your company

Map your test record sheet and run log to the columns the workflow template expects, and set the case kinds your acceptance tests use. Have the pilot owner write the bar before the pilot starts, in measures the template can check, such as a share of test runs passed or a number of real runs; anything else is left for the leader to judge. Set the few-cases line, which is 10 by default. Name who runs the write-up, who decides, and who will hold each of the four responsibilities after the pilot. Whether the workflow is then used and useful across the team is measured later, as [Installed, used or useful?](https://fullypowered.ai/insights/installed-used-or-useful/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=workflow-collections&utm_content=operations-ai-pilot-result-write-up&from=gh-operations-ai-pilot-result-write-up) sets out. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the files you supply; it needs no connection to any system.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
