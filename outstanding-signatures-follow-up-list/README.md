## The outcome

Nothing sits unsigned because nobody noticed. Every document still out for signature appears once, with the person it is waiting on, how long it has been with them and how long since they last touched it. Where documents are signed in a set order, the list names the one signer holding everyone behind them, so the follow-up goes to the right person instead of the whole chain.

Documents about to expire are pulled to the top before the signing request lapses, and documents that were declined or have already expired are set apart as decisions for the person who sent them. For every signer a reminder is due to, under your team's own rules, a short reminder is ready for the sender to read, adjust and send. A count at the top ties every document and every row back to the export, so nothing drops out between the tool and the list.

It reads the status export only, never the documents themselves. It sends no reminder, resends nothing, voids nothing and changes no record: a person does each of these. It says nothing about whether a signature is valid or a document binding.

## Who starts the work, and when

The operations coordinator, the office manager or the sales operations person who sends documents out for signature runs it twice a week, and again in the last days of the month, when unsigned order forms and renewals cost the most. Each run starts from a fresh status export, so the list always reflects what the tool shows that morning.

Each sender approves the reminders for their own documents and decides what happens to anything declined or expired.

## What they supply

- **The status export from your e-signature tool**, one row for each signer of each document, with the columns document_id, document_name, document_type, sender, sent_at, recipient, recipient_role, signing_order, status (sent, viewed, signed, declined, expired or voided), last_activity_at and expires_at. If a column is named differently, or a status word is one the list does not know, the work stops and asks. It also stops and asks when a document and signer appear twice, the rows of one document disagree, signing order mixes blank and numbered, or activity is dated before sending.
- **Your team's follow-up rules:** after how many days without activity a reminder is due, which document types are urgent and get a shorter wait, how many days ahead to warn of an expiry, and, for each kind of signer, who may remind them, or that they are never reminded from this list.
- **Today's date,** when the export was taken, and who runs the list: the name or role that appears on it and in its questions.

## What comes out

One follow-up list in a fixed order: what is waiting for a signature, with the signer each document waits on and the days in hand; what expires within the warning window; what was declined or has expired, as a decision for the sender; a drafted reminder for each signer due one, marked with who sends it; what is closed and needs nothing; and numbered questions. A count at the top shows every document and every row of the export accounted for.

Each reminder states only what the export shows: the document's name, who sent it, when it reached the signer and when the request expires. It carries no signing link; the sender sends it from the tool or adds the link.

## What has to change for your company

Map your tool's export columns to the ones the workflow template expects, and list the status words your tool uses. Write down your follow-up rules once: the wait before a reminder, the document types that are urgent, the expiry warning window, and for each signer role who may remind them, so a client's executive is never chased by the wrong person. Agree who answers the questions the list raises. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the export you supply; it needs no connection to your e-signature tool.
