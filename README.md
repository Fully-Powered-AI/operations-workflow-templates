**Quick start**

1. **Turn one walkthrough into a written procedure.** Open ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Copy the text below into a new chat. Replace the square brackets with your own details, then paste in a walkthrough of one real piece of work. A note, a transcript or a few typed lines will do.

   ```text
   You help a team write a standard operating procedure for ONE piece of
   work, from an account of how it is done today. You draft. People decide.
   Never send anything, change any system or mark the draft approved. The
   owner approves it.

   Procedure: [name]. Owner: [role]. Who does the work: [role]. Limits
   already set: [amounts, deadlines, or "none"]. Use roles, not names.

   Walkthrough: [paste it here]

   Use only what the walkthrough says. Never add a step, limit or deadline
   of your own. Write: purpose, scope, roles, what is needed first,
   numbered steps (one role each, and how we know it is done), exceptions,
   stop conditions, records, open questions and revision history. Where
   something is missing or two accounts disagree, write [To confirm] and
   ask the owner.
   ```

2. Read what comes back. It marks what it checked and what is left for you. What comes out:

   ```text
   **STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE · Handling a return of an item damaged in transit**

   Status: Draft for the owner's approval. Not in use until approved.

   - **Owner:** operations lead. **Prepared by:** operations lead. **Date:** 9 November 2026.
   - **Written from:** walkthrough with the returns coordinator, 3 November 2026 (W); note from the customer service lead, 5 November 2026 (N); request and local settings from the operations lead, 9 November 2026 (L).
   - **Version:** Draft 1. **Next review:** every 12 months (L).
   - **Summary:** 15 steps, 2 exceptions, 4 stop conditions, 18 open questions.

   **1. Purpose**

   Sets out how a customer's claim for an item damaged in transit is checked, settled by replacement or refund, claimed back from the carrier and recorded.
   ```

3. Stop there. You will know if this is for you. ## The operations workflow templates here today

- **[Business continuity plan](business-continuity-plan/)**: For an owner or operations manager: the activities the business cannot stop for long, how long each can be down and how much data it can lose, what each depends on, the fallback for each, and named owners, deputies, contacts and a test date, ready for a competent reviewer and the owner's approval.
- **[Client service level report](client-service-level-report/)**: At the end of each reporting period, the service delivery manager gets the report a client contract calls for: every service level measured from your own records, each miss explained by those records, the credits the contract states listed for a person to confirm, and the open questions. Nothing is sent.
- **[Incident investigation and corrective actions](incident-investigation-and-corrective-actions/)**: After an injury, near miss or property damage, the site, operations or safety manager gets the investigation written up from the evidence: a timeline, the immediate and underlying causes, actions with owners and dates, and a register that follows each action until a check shows it worked.
- **[Operations KPI scorecard](operations-kpi-scorecard/)**: Once a month the operations manager gets one red-amber-green page for the ten or fewer measures that run the operation: each value worked out from your own exports, its trend, what moved and why in the owner's words, and the action agreed for every red. You set the measures and decide the actions.
- **[Outstanding signatures follow-up list](outstanding-signatures-follow-up-list/)**: Twice a week and before month end, whoever sends documents for signature gets one list from the e-signature tool's status export: what is waiting, on whom and for how long, what expires soon, what was declined or expired, and a reminder drafted for each waiting signer. Nothing is sent.
- **[Pilot acceptance-test preparation](pilot-acceptance-test-preparation/)**: Go into every AI pilot with the test already written: the cases that matter, what good looks like for each and the rule your owner will use to decide.
- **[Premortem before a decision](premortem-before-a-decision/)**: Before your team commits to a plan that is hard to undo, such as a move, a launch or a new system, a short workshop pictures it a few months on, already failed. The record ranks the reasons, turns each top risk into a check, a warning sign and an owner, and leaves the go decision with the owner.
- **[Preventive maintenance plan](preventive-maintenance-plan/)**: For one site, the facilities or operations manager gets every piece of equipment ranked by failure cost, an approach for each (run to failure, scheduled or condition-based), a dated calendar of tasks from the maker's guidance and the failure history, the spare parts to hold and the downtime measures to track.
- **[Process map and bottleneck review](process-map-and-bottleneck-review/)**: Map one process step by step: who does each step, how long work waits and how long it takes, where it is handed over and where it gets sent back. Then find the one step that caps how much gets finished, and what to try there first. The owner decides what changes.
- **[SOP exception capture](sop-exception-capture/)**: Turn what your most experienced person does with the awkward cases into an exception register for one procedure. Once the owner approves it, every colleague handles those cases the way your best person does.
- **[Write an SOP from how the work is done](sop-from-how-the-work-is-done/)**: Turn a walkthrough of how one piece of work is really done into a written procedure anyone on the team can follow: who does each step, how they know it is done, what to do when it goes wrong and when to stop and ask. The owner approves it before anyone relies on it.
- **[Workflow fit check: is this piece of work ready for AI?](workflow-fit-check/)**: Before you build anything, run one piece of work through six checks and get a straight answer on one short page: build it, build one part, do something simpler first, not yet, or leave it with your people. The owner decides what gets built first, with the reasons in front of them.

## How to use one

Open a folder and start with its README. Every folder stands alone, so copy the one you want and leave the rest. The templates are written for ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini: paste the instruction block and the output format in as custom instructions, or load the folder in a product that reads `SKILL.md` packages.

To take the whole collection, press **Use this template** at the top of this page: it copies every folder into a repository in your own account, yours to change.

In every template a person starts the work, supplies the inputs and decides. The template prepares and checks. Anything that sends, posts, pays or changes a record waits for a person to approve it.

## What SKILL.md is

Each folder holds a file named `SKILL.md`. It is the technical file: the same instructions and output format as the template, in the packaged form that AI products which load instructions from a folder read. It is generated from `TEMPLATE.md`, so the two always agree. The workflow template is the whole folder; `SKILL.md` is one way of loading it. If your AI product does not read that format, use `TEMPLATE.md`.

## What "checked" means here

Each folder says exactly what was checked and when: who wrote it, that its sources and rights were reviewed, the date of its editorial review, and whether it has been tried on its example in a named AI product. Nothing is described as tested for a client unless it was. Test a template on your own past work before your team relies on it; each one comes with acceptance tests for that.
